NYC Schools Atlas

About this atlas

A reference for parents, journalists and researchers: every K–12 school in New York City, each set against similar schools on every measure the city and state publish for all schools at once.

The map

The map plots 2,445 schools: 1,622 district public schools, 284 charter schools and 539 private schools. Click a public or charter school to open a sheet with about 35 measures, grouped the way Chalkbeat and The City Reporter’s guide to vetting a school groups them: teacher trust in the principal and whether teachers would recommend the school, chronic absence, test scores and DOE’s impact score, graduation, school climate and safety from the 2026 school survey, class size, counselors, spending, PTA money, space, enrollment, demographics and special education services. Each measure sits on a small chart of every school at the same level, so a number comes with its context. Schools reviewed by Insideschools link to the review, with its date.

The map also lines up every school in view on every measure at once, colors schools by any one measure, and finds the zoned schools for an address. Private schools report none of these measures to the city or state, so they appear with name and location only.

Every measure’s source, year, coverage and definition is listed in MEASURES.md, and how schools are compared is explained in METHODOLOGY.md, including an Oct. 7, 2026 correction: until then the map showed the share of students who were not chronically absent under the label “chronic absence.”

The explainers

Five companion pages cover the citywide systems the schools sit inside. Each cites its sources on the page.

  • Curriculum — what New York State expects students to know, by subject and grade, from the state’s learning standards.
  • School day — how much time New York City students spend in school in 2026–27, and how that compares with the national average, Chicago and two charter networks.
  • Enrollment — how enrollment in district, charter and private schools has changed since 1976–77, with 3-K and pre-K, grade by grade, chronic absence and school staffing.
  • Spending — what the city spends per student, where the money goes and how that compares with other districts.
  • Special education — what the city spends on special education due process cases (private school tuition and services families win or settle for), and where the money goes.

Principles

Sources before claims. Every number traces to a published file, saved in the repository with the script that reads it. Where a figure is computed, the formula is written down.

Check meaning, not just matching. On Oct. 7, 2026, every value on the map was re-derived from the raw files with code written separately from the build. No value differed from its source, but the check turned up labels and comparisons that didn’t mean what they said; those were fixed and are listed in the methodology.

Visible limits. Charter schools are missing from several DOE reports; DOE redacts small counts; some measures are a year or two old; Insideschools reviews are often several years old. The map and the methodology say so where it matters.

How to use it

  • Looking for a specific school? Search the map by name, neighborhood or address, or enter an address to see its zoned schools.
  • Comparing schools near you? Zoom the map to your area and press “Line up schools in view.” Shift-click schools anywhere to pin them to the top of that list.
  • Linking to a school? Add #school= and the school’s DOE code to the map’s address, for example #school=13K307.

Build and code

The atlas is a static site: HTML, JavaScript and JSON, with no backend and no analytics. The data files are built by Python scripts in the repository from the saved source files. The map uses Leaflet and CARTO tiles; charts use Chart.js or Recharts.
