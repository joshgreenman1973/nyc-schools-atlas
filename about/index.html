NYC Schools Atlas

About this atlas

A reference for parents, journalists, and researchers — the map of every K–12 school in New York City, plus the citywide systems those schools sit inside.

What’s here

The atlas has six sections. They’re built to answer different questions but share one underlying premise: every claim is sourced and every assumption is visible.

  • Map — every NYC public, charter, and private K–12 school plotted, with demographics, programs, attendance zones, outcomes, and enrollment trajectories. 2,524 schools.
  • Curriculum — what New York State expects every K–12 student to know, by subject and grade, from the official Next-Generation and Framework standards.
  • School day — the 2026–27 calendar, the 129-hour annual instructional gap with the U.S. average, and where the time goes.
  • Enrollment — 85 years of K–12 enrollment, 1940–2025: the Baby Boom expansion, the 1970s collapse, the pre-pandemic peak, and the post-pandemic loss of 300,000 students.
  • Spending — where the $40B Department of Education budget goes, why per-pupil spending exceeds $35,000, and how that compares to the rest of the country.
  • Special education — the $1.3B Carter/Connors due-process line, growing 28× since FY2005, and what the data does and doesn’t show.

Principles

Sources before claims. Every chart and tooltip cites its provenance. Where the source is multiple files, each section’s "Sources" block lists them. The map’s per-school records carry a quality_meta field that records the exact NYC DOE file and vintage behind every outcomes metric.

Visible assumptions. Where a calculation involves a choice (a denominator, a window, a definition), it’s called out. The map’s "Enrollment trajectory" view, for example, defines “collapsing” as >25% loss over the past three reported years — not a hidden threshold.

Honest limitations. Each section has a "Honest limits" or equivalent block listing what is not in the data and why. Examples: the public DOE demographic snapshot is stale at 2021–22; private schools have no demographic reporting; DOE cannot disaggregate Carter from Connors spending; standards are not curriculum.

Per-school spending is missing. NYC DOE’s School Based Expenditure Report is not on the Open Data API in usable form. The Spending section answers the citywide question (where the $40B goes); per-school dollar amounts will be added when a clean source is available.

How to use it

  • Looking for a specific school? Use the Map — search by name, neighborhood, or address. The address bar returns the zoned schools for any NYC address.
  • Looking at trends? The Enrollment and Spending sections cover decades. The map’s "Enrollment trajectory" view shows three-year change school by school.
  • Writing about NYC schools? Every page’s charts and quoted figures are sourced. Deep-link by URL: /?school=02M212 on the map page opens that school’s card.
  • Parent or guardian? The Map’s school tooltips show demographics, programs, admissions, attendance zones, outcomes, and nearby alternatives. The Curriculum section shows what the state expects at each grade.

Build and code

The atlas is a static site — HTML, JavaScript, and JSON. No build step, no backend, no analytics. The map uses Leaflet and CARTO Dark Matter tiles; charts use Chart.js or Recharts depending on section. Fonts: Fraunces (display) and Inter (body).

Source code and data: github.com/joshgreenman1973/nyc-schools-atlas. Built by Josh Greenman. Issues, corrections, and data leads welcome.
