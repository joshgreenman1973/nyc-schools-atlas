What NYC kids are expected to know, K through 12

Curriculum varies wildly between schools. Standards don’t. Here’s what New York State says every student should be able to do by the end of each major grade band — in the four core subjects, distilled from the official NYS Next-Generation and Framework standards.

Why this exists Parents and journalists routinely encounter a gap: every NYC school teaches reading, math, science, and social studies, but what they teach (the curriculum, textbooks, sequence) can look completely different. The state-set standards are the constant. They define what a student is supposed to be able to do, regardless of which curriculum got them there. Click any cell to see a concrete example.

Sources & provenance

Every cell here is paraphrased from the published NY State learning standards. The standards documents themselves are the authoritative source — this page is a navigational distillation, not a replacement.

  • English Language Arts: NYS Next Generation ELA Learning Standards (adopted 2017, implementation 2020–21).
  • Mathematics: NYS Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards (adopted 2017).
  • Science: NYS P-12 Science Learning Standards (NYSSLS, adopted 2016; based on NGSS).
  • Social studies: NYS K-12 Social Studies Framework (revised 2014, with field guide).

Honest limitation: standards are not curriculum. A school that exceeds standards looks very different from one that barely meets them, even if both are technically “on grade level.” This page tells you the floor, not the ceiling.
