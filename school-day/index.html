New York City’s 2026–27 schedule adds up to more than a full school year less time in school over K–12 than the national average

State law requires 180 days of school. The 2026–27 calendar counts three staff training days toward that total, so students attend 177, and the standard school day of 6 hours 20 minutes is about 35 minutes shorter than the national average. For elementary and middle school students, 24 of the year’s 40 school weeks run five full in-person days; for high school students, 19 do.

The 110-hour gap

Scheduled hours in school per year: a New York City public school student in 2026–27 vs. the typical U.S. public school (national survey data from 2017–18)

New York City public schools, 2026–27: 1,121 hours. Typical U.S. public school, 2017–18: 1,231 hours. Gap: 110 hours per year.
110 hours a year is about 17 New York City school days. Over a 13-year K–12 career on this schedule, it adds up to about 1,430 hours, or about 1.2 years of school at the national average of 1,231 hours a year. Chalkbeat’s figure for 2025–26, a year with 176 school days and a snow day, was 1,102 hours.

Two factors set the total hours

School day length × student attendance days = scheduled hours per year. State education law and the city’s contract with the United Federation of Teachers shape both.

The Monday finish
The 2026–27 school year ends on Monday, June 28, 2027, the only school day that week. Chalkbeat called it “a dog-leg day that’s a recipe for low attendance.” New York City Public Schools says state law and its staff contracts require the year to end before the second-to-last weekday in June.
