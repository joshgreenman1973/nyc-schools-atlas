# Methodology: per-pupil K-12 spending in New York City

Last updated: Oct. 7, 2026

This note explains where every number on the spending page comes from, how each calculated figure was produced and what each figure does and does not include. Fiscal years (FY) are New York City's, July to June: FY2024 is the 2023-24 school year. Dollar figures are dollars as spent in the year shown unless they are labeled as adjusted for inflation.

## Sections 1 and 2: the Census series

### What the Census per-pupil figure measures

Census Table 18 reports current spending per pupil. We reproduced it exactly from the district file:

per pupil = (TCURELSC - V91 - V92) / V33

where TCURELSC is current spending for elementary-secondary education, V91 is payments to private schools, V92 is payments to charter schools and V33 is fall enrollment. For New York City in FY2024: (34,591,210 - 916,029 - 3,409,570) thousand / 845,509 = $35,795.73, matching Table 18. The same formula matches Table 18 to the dollar for the District of Columbia, Atlanta, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Chicago and Detroit, which is why it was used for Boston.

So the per-pupil numerator is current spending excluding payments to charter and private schools, and the per-pupil figure times the printed enrollment equals that numerator. Census Table 16's "current spending" column ($34.97 billion for FY2024) is larger because it also includes those payments and nonelementary-secondary programs; it is not the per-pupil numerator.

Current spending includes salaries and employee benefits (including employer pension contributions and health insurance), purchased services and supplies. It excludes capital outlay and debt service. Census excludes charter schools whose charters are held by nongovernmental entities; no New York charter school appears in the Census file. Enrollment comes from the federal Common Core of Data; for New York City it equals the sum of fall enrollment reported for the 32 geographic-district agencies in the Common Core of Data (the District 75 agency reports none separately) (845,509 in fall 2023, of whom 39,025 were pre-K). Note: the FY2024 Table 18 footnote says enrollments are fall 2022 counts, but the FY2024 New York City enrollment (845,509) differs from FY2023's (847,030) and equals the fall 2023 Common Core count, so the footnote appears to be carried over from the prior year.

New York City ranked first in Table 18 every year from FY2016 to FY2024 except FY2021, when Boston ($31,397) ranked ahead of New York City ($29,931).

### Decomposition, FY2016 to FY2024

- Per pupil rose $11,687 (+48.5 percent) as spent. Spending rose 27.9 percent; enrollment fell 13.9 percent (136,158 students). The FY2020-FY2022 drop (97,120) is 71.3 percent of the total drop.
- Counterfactual 1, FY2024 spending over FY2016 enrollment: $30,265,611 thousand / 981,667 = $30,831. Denominator effect $35,796 - $30,831 = $4,965, or 42.5 percent of the rise.
- Counterfactual 2, FY2016 spending over FY2024 enrollment: $23,666,714 thousand / 845,509 = $27,991. Denominator effect $3,882, or 33.2 percent. (A log split gives 37.8 percent.)
- After inflation (FY2024 dollars): per pupil rose $5,600 (+18.5 percent) and total spending rose 2.1 percent. The denominator share is 88.7 percent by counterfactual 1 and 86.8 percent by counterfactual 2.
- FY2021 to FY2022: per pupil +20.0 percent, spending +13.0 percent, enrollment -5.9 percent. FY2022 to FY2023: spending -8.4 percent.
- Salaries and benefits: Table 18 FY2024, $16,218 + $8,943 = $25,161, 70.3 percent of $35,796.
- IBO's broader series (2025 dollars): full agency cost $36.55 billion (FY2016) to $41.28 billion (FY2024), +12.9 percent; IBO enrollment 1,169,730 to 1,106,649, -5.4 percent; per pupil $31,247 to $37,299, +19.4 percent. Denominator share 29 to 33 percent depending on direction (31 percent by log split).
- Federal aid (IBO, 2025 dollars): $3.29 billion (FY2021), $6.14 billion (FY2022), $4.44 billion (FY2023), $4.93 billion (FY2024), $2.80 billion (FY2025).

## Section 7: the gap with the national average

- Gap: $35,796 - $17,619 = $18,177 (Census FY2024; national figure from Table 8).
- By object (Table 18 and Table 8): salaries $16,218 vs. $9,685; benefits $8,943 vs. $4,243; other $10,635 vs. $3,691. Gap: $6,533 + $4,700 + $6,944.

- Special education illustration. Assume per-pupil cost = b x (1 + 0.9s), where s is the share of students with disabilities and 0.9 is the excess cost implied by SEEP's ratio of 1.90 ("the total expenditure to educate the average student with disabilities is an estimated 1.90 times that expended to educate the typical regular education student"). With s = 15.2 percent (U.S.) and 22.4 percent (DOE, 2023-24): at the U.S. cost base, b = $15,499 and the extra share adds $1,004 (5.5 percent of the gap); at New York City's cost base, b = $29,790 and it adds $1,930 (10.6 percent). With s = 23.5 percent (CRDC 2021-22), the figures are $1,158 (6.4 percent) and $2,207 (12.1 percent). Limits: SEEP's ratio is national and from 1999-2000; the share measures differ in age range and denominator; the calculation is illustrative.

## Limitations

1. Census and IBO measure different things. Neither is "the" per-pupil number.
2. The Census series is consistent in definition, but what flows through it changed: 3-K and pre-K expansion, the class-size law and federal pandemic aid all affect spending in some years. Census does not explain year-to-year swings, and neither does this page beyond what IBO reports.
3. IBO's FY2025 enrollment count was refined by the city, which IBO says makes comparisons with FY2024 difficult.
4. Inflation adjustment uses a consumer price index. School costs are mostly wages, so a wage-based deflator could show a different real trend.
5. Special education costs are spread across many lines; no public total exists.
6. No outcomes data. The page reports spending only.
